VisionRedact Studio

I built a Windows desktop app that uses AI to find faces in video and blurs them before the video is shared. This series covers what I designed, what went wrong, what I measured, and what each fix cost.

The whole workflow in 78 seconds, with subtitles. Sound on. Trailer on LinkedIn

What video redaction is for

Before a video is shared, people who never agreed to appear in it often have to be hidden:

  • Courts and legal casesEvidence shared with the other side or shown in public.
  • Police and body camerasFootage released to the public.
  • CCTV and securityWhen footage is handed over, for example to someone who asked for footage of themselves, other people who can be identified often need to be hidden, depending on the circumstances.
  • Schools, hospitals and workplacesRecordings of children, patients or staff.
  • News and researchBystanders who never agreed to appear.

By hand, that means drawing over every face in every frame, and my 23-minute test recording (59.94 fps) has 82,373 frames. In VisionRedact, the AI finds the faces on the laptop's GPU, without uploading anything, and a person checks them. Every face it finds stays blurred until someone rejects it.
